# MIDNIGHT SEMESTER — Story Bible

A complete narrative design document: timeline, characters, act structure, level-by-level story beats, collectible lore, and multiple endings.

---

## 1. LOGLINE

A final-year engineering student stays behind alone on campus to finish a project during semester break — and discovers the university has been quietly finishing something else since 2005.

---

## 2. THE WORLD

**University:** Ravenswood Institute of Technology (fictional — invented name/crest/layout, not based on any real institution)
**Location:** Fictional campus on the outskirts of a mid-sized Indian city, built in the early 1980s, expanded rapidly in the 2000s.
**Current year (in-game):** 2026
**Player character:** Aarav Mehta (name changeable in settings), 21, final-year Data Science student, staying in the mostly-empty hostel over break to finish a capstone project that's due in three days.

---

## 3. TIMELINE (world history — player pieces this together, not told upfront)

- **1984** — Ravenswood founded. Old Academic Block (Block A) built first; still standing, mostly disused now.
- **1999** — A private research grant funds an "Applied Cognition Lab" in Block A's basement — publicly framed as AI/behavioral research, actually studying sleep deprivation and sensory isolation on volunteer students (unethical, unauthorized, kept off official records).
- **2004** — A student, **Meera Iyer**, volunteers for the lab's final trial round to pay off hostel fees. She does not leave the trial in the state she entered it.
- **2005 (the Incident)** — Meera disappears from the hostel records. Official story: she "left the program early and transferred." Block A basement is sealed and reclassified as a storage area. The lab's lead researcher, **Dr. Verma**, resigns quietly the same month.
- **2005–2012** — A string of unexplained hostel incidents (sleep paralysis reports, missing-persons rumors, a night watchman who quit without notice) get written off as "old building, bad wiring, hostel folklore."
- **2012** — Block A is officially closed for "structural renovation" that never happens. Fenced off, keys reassigned only to facilities staff.
- **2026 — present day** — Player, working late on a data-science capstone that happens to touch old university sensor logs, stumbles onto fragments of the sealed research data and gets locked into the closed wing after a power failure cuts main-gate access.

---

## 4. KEY CHARACTERS

**Aarav Mehta (Player)** — practical, deadline-stressed, not a "chosen one" — an ordinary student in the wrong building at the wrong time. His only real advantage is what he studies: data patterns, which becomes the mechanic for late-game puzzles (reading corrupted lab logs).

**Meera Iyer (Presence)** — the trial subject from 2005. Not a traditional ghost/monster in the jump-scare sense for most of the game — she's an absence: heard before seen, glimpsed at corridor ends, associated with specific sounds (a metronome, a recorded voice counting). What she becomes physically is revealed only in Act 3.

**Dr. Verma (via recordings/documents only, never appears directly)** — the lab's lead researcher. Left behind logs, personal notes, and a hidden confession recording. Morally gray: not a cartoon villain, a man who convinced himself the ends justified small, escalating compromises.

**Professor Kulkarni (radio contact)** — a current-day faculty member the player can reach by campus intercom/walkie in select rooms; provides hints, external perspective, and — depending on player choices — becomes either a lifeline or someone who was closer to the cover-up than they first let on (this ambiguity resolves differently per ending).

**The Watchman (background lore only)** — the 2005-era night watchman who quit; his logbook is a key collectible item found late in the Hostel level.

---

## 5. THEMES

- Institutional silence — how systems protect themselves over people.
- Isolation and academic pressure (deliberately mirrors the player's own late-night, deadline-driven situation — the horror is partly "what if the system doesn't care if I disappear either").
- Memory and testimony — most of the story is told through what people *recorded* and *did not report*.

---

## 6. ACT STRUCTURE

### ACT 1 — Arrival (Main Gate + Quad + Hostel intro)
Player arrives on a rainy evening to work on the capstone. Establish normalcy: near-empty hostel, one or two other late-staying students (heard, not seen — a light under a door, a radio playing, footsteps in another wing — they're never met, reinforcing isolation). A campus-wide power fault locks the main gate's electronic exit and reroutes backup power — inexplicably — toward Block A, which shouldn't have power routed to it at all. This is the inciting incident: the player has to go through Block A to reach the generator room and restore gate access.

### ACT 2 — Descent (Academic Block + Library + Hostel deep exploration)
Player finds fragmented lab documents referencing "Subject M." Begins hearing/seeing traces of Meera. Radio contact with Professor Kulkarni begins here — he claims to just be a facilities liaison who can guide the player out, but his knowledge of Block A is oddly specific. Library level: player finds archived (non-digital) newspaper clippings and a redacted disciplinary file, starts connecting the 2005 disappearance to what's happening now. Hostel deep-exploration reveals the Watchman's logbook, describing his final week before quitting, and the first real physical encounter with the Meera-presence (chase sequence, not lethal — establishes her behavior pattern for late-game AI).

### ACT 3 — Basement / Applied Cognition Lab (Finale)
Player reaches the sealed basement lab. Full picture revealed via Dr. Verma's confession recording and the lab's physical remains: Meera was never fully released from the trial's final sensory-isolation phase — a technical/procedural failure (not overtly supernatural framing until the very end) left her in a prolonged state the lab covered up rather than reported, and the "incident" was a cover-up of institutional negligence rather than of anything deliberately malicious — which makes it worse, not better. The final confrontation is not combat — it's a choice-driven sequence (see Section 8).

## 7. LEVEL-BY-LEVEL STORY BEATS

| Level | Story Beat | Key Collectible |
|---|---|---|
| Main Gate/Quad | Power fault, gate locked, first radio contact with Kulkarni | Campus map with Block A marked "OFF LIMITS" |
| Academic Block | First lab document fragments, first indirect Meera cue (recorded metronome sound) | Dr. Verma's early research memo |
| Library | Newspaper clippings on the 2005 disappearance, redacted disciplinary file | Meera's original hostel admission form |
| Hostel (deep) | Watchman's logbook, first direct Meera encounter/chase | Watchman's resignation letter |
| Basement/Lab | Dr. Verma's full confession recording, final trial equipment | Meera's personal effects (bracelet, ID card) |

Each level's collectibles are optional but combine into a "case file" the player can review at any save point — full completionists get the whole picture; players who rush through get a leaner, more ambiguous version of events. This is intentional: horror lands harder with some gaps left for the player's imagination.

---

## 8. ENDINGS (multiple, choice-driven in the final act)

**Ending A — "Report It"**
Player exits with physical evidence (the recording + documents) and chooses to hand it to campus authorities/press rather than Professor Kulkarni. Epilogue: news headline implies an investigation opens. Bittersweet — the truth gets out, but too late for Meera. Requires collecting most case-file items.

**Ending B — "Trust Kulkarni"**
Player hands the evidence to Kulkarni instead. Epilogue is ambiguous — a shot of the evidence being quietly filed away, hinting he may be more entangled in the cover-up than he claimed. Darker, "nothing changes" ending.

**Ending C — "Stay"**
A hidden-late-game option: instead of leaving, the player can choose to stay with Meera's presence in the lab in her final moments (an emotional, non-violent choice sequence). Epilogue implies the player never officially leaves campus. The most unsettling ending, reserved for players who explore deeply enough to understand who/what she actually is before making the choice.

**Ending D — "Run" (default/rushed playthrough)**
Player who skips most collectibles just escapes through the generator room without engaging with the full story. Epilogue is short and unresolved — deliberately unsatisfying, nudging completionist replays.

---

## 9. DIALOGUE/RECORDING DESIGN NOTES

- Dr. Verma's confession recording should be the emotional centerpiece — written as a man justifying himself more than confessing, so the player has to read between the lines rather than being told what happened outright.
- Kulkarni's radio lines should stay genuinely ambiguous through Act 2 — avoid writing him as obviously untrustworthy; the player's suspicion should come from environmental evidence, not dialogue tells.
- Meera has almost no direct "voice" until Act 3 — her presence should be entirely environmental/audio (the metronome motif, counting numbers) until the very end, where a short, sparse, non-monologuing voice line lands harder for having been withheld so long.

## 10. OPTIONAL EXPANSION HOOKS (post-launch content)

- A prequel DLC playable as Meera during the 2004 trial itself (found-footage/log style, shorter format).
- New Game+ mode with all case-file documents visible from the start, changing the horror from "discovery" to "dread of what you already know is coming."
- Multiple-endings achievement/epilogue gallery unlocked after first completion.
